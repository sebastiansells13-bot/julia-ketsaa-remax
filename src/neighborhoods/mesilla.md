Mesilla has a longer history than almost anywhere else in the region. After the Mexican-American War, residents who wanted to remain Mexican citizens settled here, on what was then the Mexican side of the Rio Grande. The Gadsden Purchase brought the town into the United States, marked by a flag-raising on the plaza in 1854, and its historic core has been a National Historic Landmark district since 1961.

The plaza is still the heart of town. The Basilica of San Albino anchors one side, and the surrounding blocks are lined with galleries, shops, and New Mexican restaurants. The former courthouse where Billy the Kid stood trial in 1881 still stands at the plaza's southeast corner.

Homes here range from historic adobes to newer builds designed to match the town's character. Because Mesilla is its own municipality, some rules differ from Las Cruces, and renovations in the historic district may need the Town's review.
